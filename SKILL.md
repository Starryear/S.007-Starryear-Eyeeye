# Starryear-EyeEye｜背后原图 × 中间荒诞眼睛世界

Create one flattened vertical artwork in which the photograph remains visible as the full-bleed rear evidence layer and a smaller centered rectangle contains a source-derived watercolor EyeEye world. The inset must feel like an impossible interior opened inside the photograph—not an unrelated illustration pasted on top. Aim for cute, quiet, absurd, and distinctly uncanny, without gore or horror.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the source and map its decisive subjects, silhouettes, material marks, clustering, depth, light path, palette, and negative space. A style reference may influence only abstract treatment; never copy its subjects, exact composition, palette, text, logo, signature, or watermark.
2. Build one vertical canvas, preferably 2:3 unless the user specifies another ratio. Fill the entire canvas with a crop-only, proportionally scaled copy of the original photograph. Preserve its pixels: no repainting, filtering, generative extension, retouching, eye marks, or overlays except the central inset placed above it.
3. Reserve a centered portrait inset, normally 66–76% of canvas width and 68–80% of canvas height. Leave a clearly visible band of the original photograph on every side. The inset has a clean rectangular crop edge, no frame, stroke, drop shadow, torn-paper effect, label, or decorative border.
4. Reconstruct the inset from the same photograph. Keep the source's main objects recognizable through contour, proportion, attachment, surface texture, color, and clustering, but render them as tactile watercolor/gouache beings. Enforce `one body = one being = one pair of eyes`.
5. Choose one dominant spatial absurdity supported by the source: recursive nesting, a tunnel formed by repeated source bodies, impossible inside/outside depth, scale collapse toward a central void, self-containing clusters, or an endless procession into the image. A second subtle mutation is allowed only if it reinforces the same spatial idea. The inset should become more uncanny toward its center while remaining legible at the perimeter.
6. Align the inset with the rear photograph by matching the dominant subject family, palette, light direction, and broad mass placement. Do not demand pixel continuity at the inset edge; the perceptual bridge comes from source recognition and visual rhyme. Avoid a generic fantasy portal or unrelated new location.
7. Render the inset with dry-brushed watercolor and gouache, visible pigment strokes, matte paper tooth, layered translucency, granular shadows, and restrained hand-painted irregularity. Preserve deeper shadow pockets and denser crowding than a gentle children's illustration; keep the darkest region concentrated around the spatial paradox rather than blackening the whole image.
8. Add exactly two minimal dark pigment marks to every independent being. Vary dot/oval shape, spacing, tilt, size, and openness slightly according to body scale and orientation. Direct many gazes toward the impossible center or toward neighboring beings to create collective awareness. Add no mouths, brows, eye whites, realistic pupils, or extra facial features.
9. Return exactly one flattened final image. Do not return separate background/inset files, process sheets, text, or alternate compositions unless requested.

## Composition invariants

- The unchanged photograph is visible behind the inset on the top, bottom, left, and right.
- The generated scene occupies only the centered rectangle; it never replaces the full canvas.
- The inset is large enough to dominate but small enough that the photographic evidence remains unmistakable.
- The strongest impossible depth, recursion, or scale collapse sits inside the inset, usually near its optical center or lower-middle; it must not be cropped away.
- Large source-derived beings anchor the inset perimeter, medium beings lead inward, and small beings deepen the recursion. Avoid uniform rows, random confetti, and identical clones.
- The rear photograph stays realistic; the inset stays visibly hand-painted. Their contrast is intentional.

## Source fidelity and EyeEye rules

- Derive every major inset body, color family, material cue, and spatial rhythm from visible source evidence. Compress minor noise into larger relations rather than inventing unrelated props or species.
- Preserve source-specific irregularities. Pumpkins keep ribbing, stems, scuffs, and squat variation; foliage keeps branching and feathering; rocks keep fractures and weight; fabrics keep folds and tension. Do not turn everything into smooth capsules.
- Every independently bounded body has exactly one pair of eyes. No floating eyes, shared bodies with multiple pairs, eyeless background creatures, or mechanically repeated identical faces.
- Eyes remain small and hand-painted. Let their coordinated gaze generate unease; do not use giant eyes, eye sockets, realistic eyeballs, or grotesque facial anatomy.

## Tone and guardrails

- Target tonal balance: recognizable and cute at first glance; absurd and increasingly uncanny on longer viewing.
- Uncanniness should come from spatial logic, recursion, crowd behavior, impossible containment, and collective gaze—not blood, decay, injury, screaming, predation, or shock imagery.
- Allow dense clustering and deep interior shadow when supported by the photograph, but retain readable bodies, tactile color, and a controlled focal path.
- Add no unrelated people, hands, animals, buildings, props, cosmic scenery, text, numbers, titles, logos, signatures, watermarks, frames, or panel dividers.
